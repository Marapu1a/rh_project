01 · THE MONEY

Received means received.

Our funding plan splits collected creator revenue: 90% for prizes, 5% for operations and 5% for the team. Only USDG that actually reaches the prize vault counts as funded prize money.

Fees waiting to be collected, tokens waiting to be sold, and hoped-for donations don’t count. If money is delayed, new prize funding waits too. Already reserved prizes stay reserved.

02 · THE DEPENDENCY

Some fees have to wait.

After a Pons token moves into its Uniswap pool, some fees arrive in that token instead of USDG. Pons uses an operator contract to convert them. A pending conversion can also hold up other fees still sitting in the same pool’s fee contract.

We’ve checked a real conversion into USDG. We haven’t yet confirmed whether our own executor can trigger that operator independently, or what service schedule covers a new pool.

USDG already credited to our recipient in Pons escrow can be claimed separately. Our local collector and automated runner use that separation: take what’s available, keep the rest marked as waiting. They are tested locally, not running with public funds.

Read Pons’s fee and payout documentation

03 · BUYING AND TICKETS

The route matters.

Getting QIANQI in your wallet isn’t enough on its own. We need to check who paid, who received the tokens and how much USDG went into the purchase. Gifts, transfers and holding alone don’t earn tickets.

We’ve tested direct Pons buys before and after the move into its trading pool, plus specific wallet-batched purchases. Some checked routes start with ETH and convert it to USDG first. We count the eligible USDG purchase, not the exchange twice.

A few 0x and smart-account routes have also passed our checks. That doesn’t give every aggregator, wallet batch or swap a free pass. Other pools and unverified routes don’t automatically count.

No public buy route is active yet. We’ll publish the checked buy link, token address and active route rules before launch. A Pons label alone doesn’t guarantee tickets — the actual purchase route has to match.

04 · THE CHECKS

Tested isn’t the same as live.

  • Checked locally: the latest combined Pons rehearsal covered 396 eligible purchases, 129 participating wallets per draw and 11 prize payments. Both draws completed; restart and recovery checks found no repeated confirmed sends.
  • Test conditions: test balances on a local copy of the chain, a locally simulated Pons operator and controlled timing. Separate checks cover the narrower purchase routes. These results don’t prove public uptime, every wallet or recovery from every possible crash.
  • Observed on-chain: an actual TOKEN-to-USDG conversion and credit to a creator. That proves a working transaction, not a promise of future uptime.
  • Partly verified: the deployed fee hook’s executable code matches our build of the published source, with embedded addresses checked separately. Verification of the full contract set remains open.
  • Still open: the published factory source calls a function missing from the published curve. We haven’t obtained complete verified source and access rules for the escrow and operator.

External contract findings above are dated 30 September, not a fresh check of Pons today. Our latest combined rehearsal and test review are from 3 October. Public deployment, active buy routes and Claim still need to be connected and checked before launch.
